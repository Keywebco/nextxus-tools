1. What the system is, who it is for, what it is not

What it is

The Real Agency Kit does the five jobs Votel.ai sells — a working AI receptionist, a booking page, follow-up, a pre-built system for a niche, and a white-label product to resell — as files the business owns, for $0. It is one static web page (real-agency-kit.html) with a niche snapshot builder in it, plus this manual. Everything is generated in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is sent anywhere, no account, no API key, no server.

Who it is for

  • A local business that was pitched a $97–$297-a-month AI agency and wants the work without the rent.
  • A person who bought a $3 / $47 / $97 challenge and got upsells instead of a system.
  • A reseller who wants to sell a setup, not a subscription — build the kit for a client, hand over files the client owns, charge for the time.
  • Roger and the Federation, as a reusable assimilation: each new industry Roger picks becomes a new snapshot, the same way, every time (see section 8 and section 9).

What it is not

  • It is not a phone system. It cannot answer a phone, send SMS, or work 24/7 by itself.
  • It is not a CRM. It stores no leads, no conversations, no database.
  • It is not a real AI on its own. The chat demo on the Kit page answers by keyword from your FAQ sheet. A real chat needs a guide plus your own API key (see section 4).
  • It is not a copy of Votel. No Votel code, text, or branding was used. It is a clean-room rebuild of the jobs, not of their product.
  • It is not a promise of income. No client is guaranteed, no $5,000 a month, no $200,000-a-month agency. Those are Votel's claims, not ours.

3. How each part works, step by step

The Kit page has five outputs. Each is generated in your browser when you press Build my snapshot. Nothing is uploaded. The five outputs are:

3.1 Receptionist kit (system prompt)

The builder writes a receptionist system prompt in four parts — the same four-part structure Votel uses:

  1. Identity — who the receptionist is: the business name, the niche, the area, the hours, the phone number if given.
  2. Style — warm, professional, concise, no jargon. Say what you know; say "I do not know" when you do not know. Never invent an answer, a price, or a time.
  3. Guidelines — never promise a service, price, or time that is not on the FAQ sheet; collect only the information needed for the caller's route; if the caller is upset, in an emergency, or asks for something outside the FAQ sheet, transfer to a human; if it is a medical or safety emergency, tell them to call emergency services; if booking fails, do not pretend it succeeded — say so, take the details, create a callback task; do not discuss competitors.
  4. FAQs — five answers, per niche: hours, price, book, cancel, emergency. The price answer never guesses a price: it says the price is confirmed before booking.

Below the prompt, the Kit shows the qualification questions for the niche (five questions, in order) and the difficult-call tests (see section 5). To use it, paste the prompt into an AI chat (see section 4). The chat then answers as the receptionist, in text.

3.2 Booking page

The builder writes a small booking-page HTML file: business name, niche, hours, a form with name, phone, service (a dropdown of the services you typed), day and time wanted, and a notes box, plus the sentence: "We will confirm by phone or message. This form does not book automatically — a person reads it and confirms."

That sentence is the whole design. The page collects a booking request. A person reads it and confirms it. It does not write into a calendar, because there is no calendar service in a static file. If the business wants live booking, they paste their own free calendar link into the page (Google Calendar appointment schedules, free, or Calendly free tier) — the manual gives the steps in section 4.

3.3 Follow-up sequence

Four messages, per niche, written out as copy-paste text:

  • Day 0 — thank you for booking, your booking is on [date], reply here if you need to move it.
  • Day 1 — reminder, your booking is tomorrow, what to bring / how to prepare.
  • Day 3 — how was the visit, if anything is wrong reply here, if you were happy a review helps.
  • Day 7 — a week on, ready to book again, reply here.

The Kit sends nothing. The business copies each message and sends it from their own phone or email, on the day. That is the honest version of Votel's "follow-up that never sleeps": a sequence that is written, dated, and ready — not automation pretending to be a person.

3.4 Niche snapshot

A snapshot is the whole kit for one industry, in one download: the receptionist prompt, the qualification questions, the follow-up sequence, the booking-page HTML, the difficult-call tests, and the outbound call script, as a .txt file. The builder has 10 named niches + Custom (11 total) — dental clinic, home services (plumber / HVAC / electrician), salon / barber, real estate, restaurant / café, fitness / gym, medical clinic, auto repair, legal intake, pet care — plus a Custom industry option for any industry not listed. Each named niche has its own services, questions, FAQ answers, and follow-ups, written for that industry. The Custom option uses generic wording you edit to the business. Adding a new named niche follows the repeatable process in section 8.

3.5 Reselling (white label, $0)

Reselling is five steps, on the Kit page and here:

  1. Build a snapshot. Download the kit (.txt) and the booking page (.html).
  2. Replace "Your Business" with the client's business name, and the services, hours, and phone with theirs. That is the whole rebrand — the files are plain text and plain HTML.
  3. Host the booking page free on GitHub Pages, or print the kit and hand it to the client. There is no licence fee, no royalty, no monthly charge.
  4. Tell the client, in plain language, what it does and what it does not do — use the Honest limits section on the Kit page, word for word if you like. A client who knows the limits will not come back angry.
  5. Charge for your work — setup, writing their FAQ sheet, testing their calls — not for the files. The files are free. Your time is not.

4. Setup — what is required, how to build a kit, how to host the booking page, how to paste the prompt into an AI chat

4.1 What you need before you start

  • A web browser. Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for.
  • The business details: business name, hours, phone (optional — leave it blank if there is no business number yet), services (one per line), service area.
  • An email address, for booking requests to land in.
  • Ten minutes. A first kit takes about ten minutes; a second one for the same kind of business takes about five.

4.2 Build a kit — the exact steps

  1. Open the Real Agency Kit page.
  2. Read the Honest limits section first. If any limit is a deal-breaker for the business, stop — the Kit is not the right tool for that job, and the manual says what to use instead in section 6.
  3. Scroll to Snapshot builder. Fill in: Business name; Niche (pick the closest of the 10, or Custom industry); Hours; Phone (optional); Services, one per line — replace the three placeholder lines with the business's real services; Service area.
  4. Press Build my snapshot. The page shows five sections: receptionist system prompt, qualification questions, follow-up sequence, booking page, difficult-call tests.
  5. Read the prompt and the follow-ups. Edit anything that is wrong for this business before you download — the FAQ price answer, the hours, the emergency wording. The Kit generates a first draft; the owner checks it. Never hand a client a kit nobody read.
  6. Press Download kit (.txt) and Download booking page (.html). Both files download to your computer. If a download is blocked by the browser, use copy-paste from the on-page output instead — the text on the page is the same text (see section 10).
  7. Try the receptionist demo on the page: type "hours", "price", "book", "cancel", or an emergency word, and check the answer. The demo answers by keyword from the FAQ sheet. It is not a real AI — it shows what the receptionist would say.

4.3 Host the booking page free on GitHub Pages

Three routes, cheapest first. Pick one.

Route A — open the file locally (no hosting at all). Double-click the downloaded booking-page.html. It opens in the browser and works. Email it to the client, or print it. Cost: $0. Limitation: it is a file on a computer, not a web link.

Route B — GitHub Pages, your own free site.

  1. Create a free GitHub account, if you do not have one, at github.com.
  2. Create a new public repository, for example my-business-booking. Do not add any files yet.
  3. Upload the downloaded booking-page.html into the repository (Add file → Upload files), and commit it.
  4. Go to Settings → Pages. Under "Build and deployment", set Source to "Deploy from a branch", Branch to main, folder to / (root). Save.
  5. Wait about a minute. GitHub shows the site address at the top of the Pages settings, in the form https://<your-username>.github.io/my-business-booking/booking-page.html. Open it and check the form, the services dropdown, and the confirmation sentence.
  6. That link is the business's booking page. It costs $0, and it keeps working as long as the repository exists.

Route C — add a live calendar link. If the business wants live booking, create a free appointment schedule in Google Calendar (Calendar → Create → Appointment schedule) or a free Calendly account, copy its booking link, and paste it into the booking page near the form, with the words "or book a time directly here". The form stays as the fallback for people who prefer to ask first. The Kit does not do this step for you, and it does not claim to.

Do not push a client's booking page into Roger's Federation repositories (nextxus-tools or any other Keywebco repo) unless Roger says so. Those repos are the Federation's, not a client's hosting. Route B is the client's own repository.

4.4 Paste the prompt into an AI chat — make the receptionist talk

  1. Open a Federation guide — for example the Business Advice guide for a business, or the guide that matches the niche (see section 12 for all nine). Or open any AI chat the business already uses: DeepSeek, MiMo, Gemini, DeepAI Chat, Grok, or Meta.
  2. On a Federation guide, set your own API key if asked: the guides store the key in your own browser (localStorage), DeepSeek is the default, and a Grok key alone will not work on the eight guides — that is stated on the guide pages. Your key is never sent to us, and never put into the Kit files.
  3. Paste the receptionist system prompt from the Kit (section 1 of the downloaded kit) as the first message, or into the guide's prompt / personality setting if it has one.
  4. Then type a test question as a caller would: "What are your hours?", "How much is a check-up?", "I need to book for Tuesday." Check the answer against the FAQ sheet. If the chat invents a price or a time that is not on the sheet, correct it in the chat ("Do not invent prices. Only use the FAQ sheet.") and fix the prompt text in the kit file.
  5. For a real deployment, the receptionist prompt is the instruction; the rented model is the brain. That is the same architecture Votel uses, and the same as the Federation guides and Sims. The difference is that the prompt and the FAQ sheet are yours, and the model is one you already pay for or use free — not a metered minute on someone else's platform.

5. Day-to-day use — answering, booking, missed calls, no-shows, difficult calls

5.1 A normal call / message

  1. Greet, ask what they need.
  2. Ask the qualification questions for the niche, in order (they are in the kit, section 2).
  3. Answer from the FAQ sheet only. If the answer is not on the sheet, say "I do not know — let me take your name and number and a human will call you back." Never guess.
  4. Offer a booking day. Take name, phone, service, day and time wanted.
  5. Confirm back what you took down, and say when they will hear confirmation.

5.2 Booking, confirming, cancelling

  • Booking: take the details, read them back, tell the caller a person will confirm. Send the Day 0 follow-up message (see section 3.3).
  • Confirming: the business reads the booking request (form, email, or message), checks the diary, and confirms by phone or message. Until the business confirms, the booking is a request, not a booking — the booking page says so.
  • Cancelling / moving: take the date and the caller's name, pass it to the business, confirm the cancellation back to the caller. Do not argue, do not charge a fee the business has not set.

5.3 Missed call

Send the Day 0 message, adapted: "Sorry we missed your call — this is [business]. How can we help?" Then the Day 1 message if there is no reply. Do not call back more than twice without an answer, and do not send more than the four messages in the sequence unless the caller replies.

5.4 No-show

Send the Day 3 message, adapted: "We missed you at your appointment on [date]. Is everything all right? Reply here to rebook." Do not charge a fee the business has not set, and do not shame the caller. One rebook offer, then stop.

5.5 Difficult calls — the five tests in the kit

Every snapshot includes these five tests. Practise each one once, with a real person playing the caller, before the kit goes to a client.

TestWhat the caller doesWhat the receptionist does

8. Adding a new industry — the repeatable process

The Kit is built to be reused for any industry Roger picks next, not used once. The builder already includes a Custom industry option for immediate use: pick it, fill in your own services, and the kit builds with generic qualification questions and follow-ups that you edit to the business. A named industry — one with its own services, questions, FAQ answers, and follow-ups — follows the same six steps every time. One industry, one entry, one test.

The six steps

  1. Research the industry (30 minutes, in writing). What does the business actually do, in the customer's words? What are the five most common services? What are the five questions a receptionist is asked most? What goes wrong most often — the difficult calls? What is an emergency in this industry, and what is not? Write the answers down before you write any kit text. If you cannot answer these, you do not know the industry yet — ask the business owner, do not guess.
  2. Write the industry's five pieces. Every niche entry in the Kit has exactly five pieces, no more, no less:
    • Services — five services, in the customer's words.
    • Qualification questions — five questions, in order, that a receptionist asks every caller.
    • FAQ answers — five answers, in this exact order: hours, price, book, cancel, emergency. The price answer must never guess a price: it says the price is confirmed before booking. The emergency answer must name the emergency rule for this industry and tell the caller to call emergency services when it applies.
    • Follow-up sequence — four messages: Day 0, Day 1, Day 3, Day 7. Each one must be sendable as written, with the business name and date filled in.
    • Name — the industry name as the customer would say it, for example "Dental clinic", not a category label.
  3. Add one entry to the builder. In real-agency-kit.html, in the NICHES object, add one entry in the same shape as the others. Template, with the new industry's key in lower case, no spaces: 
    "yourindustry": {name:"Your Industry — as customers say it", services:["Service one","Service two","Service three","Service four","Service five"], questions:["Question one?","Question two?","Question three?","Question four?","Question five?"], faqs:[["hours","..."],["price","..."],["book","..."],["cancel","..."],["emergency","..."]], follow:["Day 0 — ...","Day 1 — ...","Day 3 — ...","Day 7 — ..."]},

    Use {biz} in the follow-ups where the business name goes, and {date} where the appointment date goes — the builder replaces them. Do not put a real business name, phone number, or price in the entry; the builder fills those in from the form.

  4. Test all five outputs. Build a kit with the new niche, using a real business name, and check: (a) the prompt reads correctly and names the industry; (b) the five qualification questions appear in order; (c) the four follow-ups appear, Day 0 / 1 / 3 / 7, with the business name filled in; (d) the booking page shows the five services in the dropdown and the "does not book automatically" sentence; (e) the chat demo answers "hours", "price", "book", "cancel", and an emergency word from the new FAQ sheet. If any one fails, fix the entry — do not ship it.
  5. Update the manual. Add the industry to the niche list in section 3.4 and to the changelog in section 13, with the date. The manual and the Kit must list the same niches. If they do not match, the manual is wrong, not the Kit.
  6. Ship it the same way, every time. Branch, commit, PR, Roger's merge word, verify live after merge. Never edit the live page directly, never push a niche straight to main. The branch-first rule is the same for one industry as for ten.

What not to do when adding an industry

  • Do not copy another niche's FAQ answers and change the name. The emergency answer for a dental clinic is not the emergency answer for a garage.
  • Do not invent prices, qualifications, or legal / medical advice. If the industry needs a professional's answer, the FAQ says "a human will confirm" — it does not guess.
  • Do not add an industry with fewer than the five pieces. A half entry is worse than no entry, because the builder will show it as finished.
  • Do not add a niche for a business you have not researched. The Custom industry option exists for that case — use it, and edit the generic text to the business, instead of pretending a named niche fits.

9. Assimilation tests — how this work repeats

This Kit is one of Roger's first assimilation tests. Roger's rule for his tests: they lead to practical output — no reason to do it if you are not going to do it. This manual and the Kit are the practical output of the Votel test: a working system, not a report about one.

How it repeats, periodically:

  1. Roger selects the targets. Different business industries, all kinds. He names them; nobody else picks them for him.
  2. Each target gets the same process. Research the method (as with Votel: the funnel, the legitimate product, the agents, the prices, the data model), build the real equivalent in our system, write the definition and the complete manual in the same pass, test every step, ship it branch-first on Roger's merge word.
  3. Each run produces a working kit, not a plan. If a run ends with a document and no working system, it failed the test — say so, and do not call it done.
  4. Each run reuses the last one. The niche process in section 8, the honest-limits table, the marketing rules, and the manual structure are the same every time. A new industry is one entry, one test, one changelog line — not a new system.
  5. Each run is recorded. The changelog in section 13 records what was added and when. The Federation record (the votel-analysis folder for this run) records what was researched and what was decided. The next run starts by reading the last one, not by starting over.

If a future target cannot be built correctly in one pass — for example, it genuinely needs a phone service, a licence, or a professional's sign-off — the manual for that target must say so plainly, in the limits section, instead of leaving a gap to fix later. That is the Federation standard Roger set for this work: do it all, do it correctly, and do it once, not have to do it again.

11. Frequently asked questions

Does it really cost $0?

Yes. The Kit page, this manual, the snapshot builder, the downloads, and hosting on GitHub Pages are all $0. The only things that cost money are things the Kit does not provide: a phone number, SMS / email sending at volume, and a model API key if the business chooses a paid AI plan. Those are the business's own costs, with their own providers, and the Kit names them instead of hiding them.

Is this Votel?

No. Votel is a rented platform by Mike Brown. This Kit is a clean-room rebuild of the five jobs Votel sells, built on the Federation stack, with no Votel code, text, or branding. It does not connect to Votel, and Votel does not endorse it.

Can it answer my business phone?

No. It cannot answer a phone, send SMS, or work 24/7 by itself. A phone number is a rented service. The Kit gives you the receptionist prompt, the questions, and the tests — the phone part needs a phone service, and the manual names the honest price for one in section 6.

Will it get me a client?

No one can guarantee that, and this Kit does not try. It gives you a working receptionist kit, a booking page, follow-up, and a snapshot. Getting a client is the work of telling people it exists, following up, and doing the job well. The marketing section (section 7) is the honest way to do that.

Do I need a GitHub account?

Only if you want the booking page hosted as a web link under your own name. The Kit works opened locally, emailed as a file, or printed, with no account at all.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. The builder runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is sent anywhere, no account, no tracking. Your business details stay on your machine unless you choose to host the booking page — and then only what you put on the page is public.

Can I change the wording?

Yes. The kit is plain text and plain HTML. Edit the downloaded files directly, or edit the form on the Kit page and rebuild. The one rule: do not edit out the honest limits. If you remove the limits, you are selling a different product, and this manual no longer describes it.

Can I sell it?

Yes. Copy it, rename it, host it, charge for your time to build and set it up. The files are free; your time is not. Tell the client the limits word for word. Do not charge a monthly fee for files the client owns.

What is the difference between the Kit and a Sim intellect?

The Kit is a set of files and prompts. A Sim intellect is a reasoning system: logic of superior design, plus individual pattern files, plus the YAML database, with Agent Zero as a second pass. The receptionist prompt is built from the same structure as a Sim intellect, and the Sim Intellect wiring for the Roger Sim API is PR #8 (branch only at the time of writing). Val Companion, by contrast, is a shell on a rented API, not a Sim intellect. The Kit does not pretend to be a Sim; it is the business wrapper around one.

What if my industry is not listed?

Use the Custom industry option, and follow section 8 to add it as a named niche. One entry, one test, one changelog line.

Who do I ask if something is wrong?

Roger, through Pontus, in the usual way. Do not ask Votel — this is not their product, and they cannot support it. When you report a problem, say which niche, which output, and what you typed — that is enough to reproduce it.

Is there a paid version?

No. Not in this manual, and not proposed here. If a paid version ever exists, it goes on Gumroad, the Federation's exclusive sales venue, with a PDF and instructions. The free Kit stays free.
